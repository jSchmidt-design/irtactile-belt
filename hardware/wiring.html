Plate 01

Control topology

wiring.md §intro

Five subsystems. The host only ever writes; the ESP32 only ever logs back on a separate wire. Everything the ESP32 commands leaves through the interface column in the middle — a relay bank for the enables, a DAC for the torque command — and the only thing that comes back from the drives is the encoder pair, through four line receivers.

Two paths, one switch

Cutting the safety switch starves the relay boards’ input optocouplers. All three relays open: drives #1 and #2 are disabled in hardware, and GPIO 19 goes HIGH, which makes the firmware declare INACTIVE and command 0 V. Neither path needs the other to work, and the hardware one is faster — the relay opens immediately, the firmware notices on its next 100 ms poll. Plate 04 draws the mechanism.

GPIO 12 is deliberately unused, and must stay that way

It is the MTDI strapping pin: it must read LOW at reset or the chip selects a 1.8 V VDD_SDIO and the flash supply is wrong. Drive 1’s enable used to live here, and a relay board’s IN pin is not high-impedance — it is the cathode of the input optocoupler LED, fed from the board’s VCC through roughly 1 kΩ, so it pulls toward 3.3 V. Against GPIO 12’s weak internal pull-down (~45 kΩ) that divider sits near 2 V, a solid logic HIGH — and the firmware’s own boot state for the pin was HIGH as well. The symptom is a board that boots with the safety switch open and unreliably or not at all with it closed, since the switch controls the very VCC that creates the pull-up. Drive 1’s enable is now GPIO 25 — no strapping or boot-log role, and four header positions from 12 on the same side of the devkit, so the relay wire still reaches. Leave GPIO 12 unconnected, and do not fit a stronger external pull-down instead: that fights the opto LED current and makes the relay’s turn-on marginal.

Plate 03

Torque command path

wiring.md §2–3

The ESP32 has no analog output, so the torque command for both drives is generated by one GP8413 dual 15-bit DAC on I²C. Both channels are written in a single auto-incrementing 5-byte transaction, once per 6 kHz tick — about 56 µs of the 167 µs budget.

The module (DFRobot Gravity, DFR1073) has only four terminals on its input side — D, C, +, −. It runs from a single 5 V rail (its datasheet supply) shared with the rest of the control side; an on-board boost converter makes the 0–10 V output, so there is no separate high-voltage analog supply.

The Vref input accepts −10 V … +10 V, but the GP8413 produces 0 … +10 V only. The drives never see a negative command, so the belt pulls in one direction per channel. That matches the firmware — processData() takes max(stream, encoder) and clamps at zero, never a signed value — and the mechanics: channel 2 gets its opposing direction from being mounted opposite-handed, not from the command sign.

TBD on this plate

External I²C pull-up value actually fitted (internal ~45 kΩ is marginal at 1 MHz) · shielded pair for the analog run, shield grounded at one end · drive parameters: control mode, full-scale voltage, gain, offset, dead band, torque limit, speed limit — and that 0 V is exactly zero torque, not a bipolar mid-scale.

AGND is not isolated

Unlike the enable path, the analog command shares a ground: both drives’ pin 13 and the GP8413 ground meet at the DAC, and through the devkit that net reaches USB ground and the host PC. The star topology removes the drive-to-drive coupling, but not the path to the PC.

Plate 04

Enable & safety chain

wiring.md §4

The ESP32 does not drive the servo-on inputs directly. Each enable GPIO drives a relay, and the relay contact sits in the drive’s enable path — the module provides both the level shift and the isolation. The safety switch is not in the coil current and not in any mains circuit: it sits in the 3.3 V supply to the relay boards’ input side, so it is an ordinary low-voltage, low-current switch with no special rating.

The VCC–JD-VCC jumper must stay removed

Separating those two rails is what puts the safety switch in the path at all. Refitting the jumper — the default state these boards ship in — would tie VCC to the unswitched 5 V and silently defeat the kill path: the relays would keep working normally, and the only symptom would be a safety switch that no longer does anything. Check this after any board swap or repair.

Fail-safe in the right direction

The contact is normally open, so a de-energised relay, a broken coil wire, a lost coil supply, an unpowered or resetting ESP32 all leave pin 6 floating and the drives inactive. Activation requires the firmware to actively hold the GPIO low. Re-arming is not automatic — when the switch returns, the firmware moves to PRE_INITIALIZED, which is only left by pressing the mode button.

Plate 05

Drive-side input circuit

wiring.md §4

The AASD-15A digital inputs are optocoupler inputs sharing one common anode terminal, with a 5 kΩ series resistor internal to the drive. This is the manufacturer’s “switch input method”: supply positive to pin 9, supply negative to COM(10), and a contact between the two energises the LED.

Why pin 9 runs at 5 V and not 12–24 V — LED forward current
Pin 9 supplyLED currentRelative
24 V≈ 4.6 mA
12 V (datasheet minimum)≈ 2.2 mA
5 V — as built≈ 0.76 mA

5 V is below the specified range on paper, but driving these inputs from a 5 V logic rail is long-established practice in the SFX-100 motion-simulator community, which has used the same AASD-15A drives this way for years across a large number of builds. The optocoupler turns on reliably at this current; the field record is the justification, and it lets the whole control side run from the single USB 5 V rail with no separate 24 V supply.

If a build ever does show marginal or intermittent enabling, the remedy needs no rework of the control side: the relay contact is dry and already isolating, so pin 9 can be moved to 12 V or 24 V while the coil stays at 5 V and the GPIO at 3.3 V. Only the wire into pin 9 and the contact’s return change.

Two drive outputs would answer questions the system cannot presently answer

SigOut1 (pin 11) is SRV-READY — the drive telling the controller it is ready. SigOut2 (pin 23) is alarm detection: a faulted drive is currently invisible to the firmware and to the log. Both are open-collector, both unwired. TBD: whether GPIO 19’s status input is fed from SigOut1 on one of the drives, or from something else entirely.

Plate 06

Encoder feedback

wiring.md §5

The source is the drive’s position pulse output on CN2 — a differential line-driver copy of the motor encoder, referenced to DGND. The line-driver pairs do not go to the ESP32 directly: each pair is received by its own small RS-485-to-TTL module, used purely as a differential line receiver. One module per signal, four in total.

Four modules, one per signal
ModuleInput A+ / B−Output RXD →CarriesPCNT behaviour
1#1 PA+ (20) / PA− (19)GPIO 4ch1 A phaseunit 0, rising edge = 1 count
2#1 PB+ (18) / PB− (17)GPIO 5ch1 B phaseB low ⇒ count down, high ⇒ up
3#2 PA+ (20) / PA− (19)GPIO 26ch2 A phaseunit 1, rising edge = 1 count
4#2 PB+ (18) / PB− (17)GPIO 27ch2 B phasemirrored: B low ⇒ count up

The firmware counts rising edges on A only, and uses the level of B at that edge to pick the direction — a 1× decode, not 4×.

Counts are never converted to distance, and do not need to be

The drives run in torque mode, so the encoder is not used to measure or command position. The count is a relative displacement from the zero set at arming, and it feeds a floor under the torque command that rises as the belt is pulled out — a virtual spring, not a position loop. That makes the counts-to-DAC gain a tuned feel parameter rather than a calibrated mapping: there is no counts-per-revolution figure to record. The one number that matters is where the floor saturates.

TBD on this plate

Confirm the exact transceiver marking (MAX3485-type assumed) · confirm only the module terminates — each pair is point-to-point, so one 120 Ω termination per pair is right; if the drive also has a termination option enabled, or a module is daisy-chained, the load halves. Keep TXD disconnected: the 74HC04 auto-direction circuit enables the RS-485 driver when TXD goes low, which would put it in contention with the drive’s own line driver.

Plate 08

Signal scaling

wiring.md §7

Two paths reach the DAC and the larger of them wins. The stream path is the tactile sample arriving over UART0. The encoder path is a torque floor that rises with displacement: value = 3 × counter × 32767 / 20000, saturated at 32767. Pulling the belt out raises the minimum force while the tactile stream still modulates above it.
